For the hard nights

Clarity

What I understood, written down so I never have to relearn it in pain. Read it when the longing comes. It is true even when it does not feel true.

Read this first

When you feel weak, when you want to text, unblock, drive by, or check, start here. Slowly.

The decision is already made

The clear version of me, the one who could think straight, already decided. It is done. Full and final. No contact, both blocked. When the urge comes, I do not renegotiate the decision. I just wait for the wave to pass. It always passes.

The five lines that are always true

  • The pull I feel is not love calling me back. It is an old alarm firing, plus grief, plus a nervous system that got used to intensity.
  • Going back leads to the same room. Warmth, then withdrawal, then the wound. I have watched it happen every single time.
  • Her coming back is not proof she changed. It is the pendulum swinging on schedule. Predictable, not meaningful.
  • It was never about my worth or my past. It was wiring set long before I arrived. Nothing I could have done would have fixed it.
  • The calm I feel when I am away from her is not boredom. It is my body telling me the truth. Trust the calm, not the craving.
"I can walk away from a book without reading the last chapter."

The pattern

Two different women. Same ending. The common thread was the fit between their wiring and mine.

The anxious avoidant trap

An anxious person and an avoidant or disorganized person find each other and lock together like a key in a lock. It is one of the most documented patterns in relationship psychology. I was the anxious pursuer. They were the distancers. That fit is the engine of everything that happened.

My side: anxious attachment

My nervous system learned that love was uncertain, so it stays alert for signs of losing it, over gives to secure connection, reads a partner's moods as threats to scan, and ties my worth to being chosen. It also reads intensity and anxiety as love, which is exactly how I kept getting drawn to unavailable people. This is the part I can actually change, and that is the hopeful part.

Their side: disorganized or fearful avoidant

Two opposite fears live in the same body. Fear of being abandoned, and fear of being trapped when closeness grows. So they pull you in, then push you away, and can never quite land. Closeness feels like danger. When it climbs too high, the alarm fires and they create distance however they can.

The freeing truth

The one person present in both relationships was me. That is not a criticism. It is the most hopeful thing here, because my own pattern is the single thing I can change, and changing it is what decides whether the next one goes the same way or not.

The eject button

When closeness climbs past what their system can hold, the fear fires and they press a button to restore distance. Different words every time. Same job underneath.

How to spot it

It comes at the peak of a good moment, not during a real problem. It is sudden, out of proportion, and it lands on my softest spot or announces an ending out of nowhere. That timing is the fingerprint. Warmth, then a rupture, right when things get close.

Example 1

The last dinner

Setup: Early days with Mahnoor, a beautiful dinner, closeness rising fast.

"This is our last dinner."

What it did: Announced an ending in the middle of a beginning. It pushed me away and pulled me to reassure her at the same time. A test and a hook.

Example 2

You deserve better

Setup: Mahnoor again, a good moment, mood glowing.

"You deserve better."

What it did: Sounds noble, works as a door slam. It put the ending on me being too good, so she never had to name the real thing, which was that closeness scared her.

Example 3

The dart at the door

Setup: Ayesha at the laundry, two warm hours, tears, I love you, me caring for her.

The sudden sexual comment about another man, right as I was leaving.

What it did: Reached for the cruelest line at the tenderest moment to restore distance. Real or invented, it changed nothing. It was aimed to wound, not to inform.

Why I should not reassure the button

Reassurance in a healthy relationship is love. But answering the eject button rewards it, so it repeats. It never lands, because the fear is inside them, not in anything I said. And it locks me into the pursuer role forever. The words cannot fix a fear that was never about me.

The cycle

Once I see the whole loop, it stops being a mystery and becomes a thing I can predict, and step out of.

1
Closeness risesIt feels good, until it climbs toward the threshold where closeness starts to feel like danger.
2
The eject buttonThe fear fires. A jab, an ending, a fight over nothing, going cold, blocking. Distance restored.
3
Relief in the distanceTheir fear quiets. They seem fine, even relieved, while I am left shattered.
4
The pendulum swings backAs distance stretches, the other fear, abandonment, takes over. The space that soothed them starts to ache.
5

6
Reconnection, then repeatWarmth floods in, closeness climbs to the same threshold, the button gets pressed again. Back to stage one.

The threshold

Everyone has a ceiling where intimacy feels like more than they can hold. Secure people have a high one. The deeper the wound, the lower it sits and the harder the alarm fires. That is why Mahnoor ejected within a single evening and Ayesha had more runway before it fired. Their ceiling, not my failure. More love does not raise it. Only their own work does.

I was the string on the yo-yo

My pursuit soothed their abandonment fear when they returned, and my closeness triggered their engulfment fear and got me ejected. The loop cannot run without a pursuer who responds. No contact cuts the string. The yo-yo cannot swing.

Why it could not work

The good things were experiences I can have again anywhere. The bad things were the foundation, and the foundation was gone.

The good column

Trips, food, movies, long drives, sunsets, late night talks, good days. Real, but every one is an experience I can have again with a safe person, or on my own. None of them was safety, trust, or being chosen.

The case, by category

  • No emotional safety. Walking on eggshells, always braced for the next flip. A braced nervous system cannot rest.
  • No trust. Dating apps and secret contact undermined the ground the whole thing stood on.
  • No commitment or clarity. Deliberate ambiguity and breadcrumbs kept me hoping just enough to stay.
  • Contempt at my soft spots. My past, my finances, my situation, used as weapons instead of protected.
  • One sided. I carried, paid, planned, gave, while being criticized for it. Not a partnership.
  • Volatility. Explosions, standoffs, good days that never lasted, my peace hostage to a mood.
  • No repair. No accountability, only rupture and return. Nothing ever mended.
  • The cost to me. Sleep, health, focus, career, peace, and years. That was the real bill.

My own verdict, in my own words

If we reunite, high chance it goes back to the same thing. And even if she talks, very low chance she stays committed, and I will not feel emotionally safe.

That was not fear talking. That was the clearest version of me doing the honest math. Trust him.

The bargaining trap

"If only she did not have this issue, it could have worked" is imagining a different person wearing her face. Her wiring ran through all of it. The partner who could love me beautifully is a secure person, and that is a future ahead of me, not a lost version of her behind me.

Hard nights toolkit

For the surges. The urge to text, the intrusive images, the jealousy, the loneliness. Pick one thing and do it.

Right now, in this moment

  • Put the phone down and stand up. Move the body. Walk, water on the face, ten pushups.
  • The surge peaks and falls within a couple of minutes if I do not feed it. Ride the wave, do not act on it.
  • Do not text. Do not unblock. Do not check. The decision is already made.
  • Message a safe friend, even about nothing. Change the room I am in.
  • Read the Start Here page again, slowly.
When I want to text her

The urge is the pain looking for relief, or the wound looking for revenge. Neither lands. If I need to say it, I write it all out here or in my notes and I do not send it. Saying it is the release. Sending it is the relapse. Silence is the cleanest last word.

When the jealous images come

It is not a memory, it is my own mind generating a picture from one cruel sentence. I do not fight it, because fighting makes it louder. I name it flatly, "that is the dart again," and turn my eyes to something real in the room. I do not investigate, do not seek certainty. Even if it were true, it changes nothing about my path. It fades fast when I stop feeding it.

When I miss her and feel lonely

Missing is real and allowed. Four years live in the body. I let the grief come in doses instead of fighting it or analyzing it. I feel it, and it moves through. Then I put my energy back into my own life. The void is not filled from outside by another person. It is filled by rebuilding my own ground.

When my mind starts analyzing her

Analysis feels like progress but it is a leash. Understanding her more does not free me, it keeps her at the center. I already have the full picture. When the decoding starts, I say "I do not need to know," and turn back to my own page. Let her be a mystery I no longer need solved.

Social media

The feed feeds me what keeps the wound open. For now I stay logged out, or I reset the feed and mark breakup content as not interested. I do not scroll toward her. Starve the algorithm and it moves on within days.

Healing my side

The one attachment future I get to shape is my own. Attachment style is learned, which means it can be relearned. This is earned security.

The work, in pieces

  • Regulate my own nervous system. When the alarm fires, soothe it from the inside. Breathe, ground, hand on chest, tell the frightened part I am safe. Become my own steady base.
  • Find the gap between trigger and reaction. Feel the urge to chase or check, and do nothing for a while. The freedom lives in that pause.
  • Build worth outside being chosen. Work, friendships, body, accomplishments. A full life means no single person can shake me.
  • Stop the over giving and rescuing. Love is reciprocal. Practice receiving and setting boundaries. My needs matter equally.
  • Relearn what safety feels like. Calm is the goal, not boredom. The flutter I called chemistry was often just the alarm.
  • Slow down in dating. Do not merge fast, do not lose myself, keep my own life running.
  • Tolerate uncertainty. Not knowing is not danger. Sit in it without writing a catastrophe.
  • Therapy, aimed here. Attachment wounds heal inside safe relationships, including with a good therapist. This is the real vehicle.

What secure love looks like (green flags)

Closeness makes them come closer, not flinch. They repair after conflict. They are consistent. My soft spots are safe with them. There is no bracing. It feels calm, and calm is the win.

Books that reinforce this

Attached, by Amir Levine and Rachel Heller. Polysecure, by Jessica Fern. Heidi Priebe on attachment and self regulation.

Expect the waves

This does not go straight down. Good day, then a hard one out of nowhere. That is not the plan failing, that is healing. On the hard nights I do not need willpower, I need one rule: the decision is already made, so I do nothing, and I let the wave pass.
